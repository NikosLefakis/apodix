Brand guidelines

How to use the Apodix name, mark, colors and voice. Short on purpose: if a rule isn't here, ask whether it helps someone trust a verdict faster.

The idea: an ending you can trust

Apodix is Greek for proof. The mark is built from the oldest construction most people know, and finished the way a proof is finished.

The shape is a Reuleaux triangle: Euclid’s first construction, where each side is an arc drawn from the opposite corner. It also reads as a seal, the kind you stamp on checked work.

The square is ∎, the symbol mathematicians put at the end of a proof. It means: shown, finished.

The i carries the same square as its dot, so the wordmark ends the way a proof does.

Logo

Use the horizontal logo by default. Use the stacked logo when the space is square, and the mark alone only when the name is already visible nearby.

Clear space and size

Keep a margin around the logo equal to the height of the ∎ in the mark (about one quarter of the mark's height). No text, edges or images inside it.

The horizontal logo should never be smaller than 24px tall on screen. Below that, use the mark alone. Below 16px, use the favicon.

Color

Ink and chalk do the work. Blue appears once per view, on the thing to act on. The three verdict colors mean exactly one thing each and are never decorative.

Typography

Three voices, each with one job. All three are free and open source.

Aa

Bricolage Grotesque

ExtraBold, tight tracking. Logo, headlines, buttons, stamps.

Aa

Source Serif 4

Regular. Paragraphs and explanations. It reads like a ledger, not a brochure.

pytest

JetBrains Mono

Commands, test names and anything the user may copy.

Supporting graphics

Three devices carry the brand beyond the logo. Use them sparingly, one per layout.

Construction lines

Two circles and a triangle, drawn thin in pale blue. Backgrounds and covers only, never behind body text.

ProvenMissing

Verdict stamps

Tilted 5 degrees, two-pixel border, sentence case. The only things in the brand allowed to rotate.

The ∎ on its own

A plain square in proof blue marks the end of something: a section, a report, a page. One per view.

Please don't

No. Stretch or squash the logo.

No. Rotate it. Only verdict stamps tilt.

No. Recolor the ∎ or use verdict colors in the logo.

No. Add shadows, outlines or gradients.

Voice

Plain, exact, evidence first. Apodix never says "probably" and never says "great job".

We say

  • “1 of 3 criteria proven.”
  • “The test still passes with the check removed.”
  • “No code or test mentions this rule.”
  • “Not done yet.”

We don't say

  • “Looks mostly good!”
  • “Your tests may possibly be weak.”
  • “AI-powered next-gen verification.”
  • “Oops, something went wrong.”
